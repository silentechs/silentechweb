# Editorial and visual audit

Reviewed the homepage, consultancy page, four project stories, shared footer, hero illustration and social cards. “AI slop” is used here as an editorial description of generic or unhelpful output, not as a claim that prose can reliably reveal its author.

## Findings and changes

| Finding | Example before | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated two-part slogans obscured the offer | “Less busywork. More progress.” | “Automation & integrations”, followed by specific tasks and the newsroom example. |
| Project titles could describe almost any agency | “An industrial vision. A digital presence to match.” | Identifies Philbro’s product catalogue and corporate website. Other case titles now identify their actual functions. |
| Unsubstantiated praise replaced useful information | “The same care”, “thoughtfully built”, “beautifully simple” | Removed or replaced with services, industries, location and portfolio details. |
| Consultancy introductions repeated the same promise | Repeated “journey”, “clear way forward” and lifecycle introductions | Describes engagement scope and expected deliverables. The homepage summary and deeper service page remain for different reading depths. |
| Illustration could imply a single integrated product suite | Commerce, media and people connected through one platform | Labels it a concept showing the kinds of workflows Silent Tech builds for. |
| Decorative elements suggested claims without evidence | Upward bar chart with arbitrary values; generic AI sparkle icon | Removed the chart and replaced the sparkle with an integration icon. |
| Sharing cards retained the old slogans | Generic headlines in generated images | Updated and regenerated all six social cards to match the revised positioning. |

## Design assessment

The warm palette, readable typography and real portfolio screenshots are worth retaining. The repeated two-tone headings, numbered sections, tilted cards and orbit illustration still resemble familiar agency templates. They are not evidence of poor quality by themselves; the more important question is whether the page makes the company and its work understandable. The editorial pass makes the section labels and project descriptions more specific without redesigning the approved layout.

The connected-card hero remains a conceptual illustration, not a product screenshot. Actual project imagery carries the proof further down the page. A future authentic team/workshop photograph could add a more personal introduction if one is available.

## Evidence still worth adding

- Named team members, their roles and verified professional experience.
- Client-approved testimonials attributed to actual people and organisations.
- Measured project outcomes with context and permission to publish.
- More project-specific constraints and decisions, based on confirmed delivery records.

No fabricated metrics, testimonials, certifications or client identities were added. The newsroom development and planned refinery remain distinguished from live public products. This pass did not independently re-verify every previously sourced product feature.

## Verification

Existing HTTP/SEO checks, lint and TypeScript validation were run after the copy edits. Desktop preview inspected for heading fit and layout. No new snapshot tests were introduced merely to lock in wording. Changes remain local.
